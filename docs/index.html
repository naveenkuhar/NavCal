Navcal

A calendar for GNOME that plans around your day.

Navcal works with the calendars you already have — Google, Microsoft 365, Nextcloud, iCloud — and brings your events, tasks, weather and plans together in one clean, fast app.

Quick add

Just type it

Press Ctrl N and write the event the way you’d say it. Navcal works out the day, the time, the place and whether it repeats, and shows you what it understood before you add it. Try it here — it works just like in the app.

  • Dates today, tomorrow, friday, next friday, sep 30, 9/30, in 3 days
  • Times 1pm, 13:00, noon, 1-2pm, from 9 to 11am, morning
  • Lengths for 2 hours, for 45 min, all day
  • Repeats every day, every monday and thursday, every other week
  • Places @ Room 4, or “at” and a name

    All your calendars, in sync

    Navcal uses GNOME’s calendar service, so the accounts you add in Settings simply show up — and changes sync both ways.

    • Google, Microsoft 365, Exchange, Nextcloud, iCloud and any CalDAV server
    • Subscribe to calendar links and add your country’s holidays
    • Import and export standard .ics files, thousands of events in seconds

    Knows where you’re going

    Type a place and pick it from the suggestions. Navcal shows the forecast for when you get there and, for cafés, shops and museums, whether they’ll be open.

    • Address suggestions as you type, from OpenStreetMap
    • The forecast at the place, at the time of the event
    • A warning on the event when a place is closed or closes before it ends

    Move it, stretch it, copy it

    Drag an event to another time or day, or drag its edge to change how long it lasts. Hold Ctrl to leave a copy behind: a faded copy follows the pointer and an outline shows where it will land.

    • Select several events to copy, cut or delete them together
    • Pinch, or scroll with Ctrl held, to zoom the hours
    • Undo your last fifty changes, even after restarting

    Print it your way

    Print a day, a week or a month as a calendar, as a checklist to tick off, or both side by side — and see exactly how it will look before you print.

    • Put the calendar left, right, above or below the agenda
    • Portrait or landscape, with or without colors
    • Save it as a PDF, or as an image to share

    A bottom bar that’s yours

    The bar under the calendar works like a dock. Choose what it holds and in what order, and turn off what you don’t need.

    • A button per calendar to show or hide it
    • The next event, tasks due today, the weather now and world clocks
    • Shortcuts to New Event, Today, Search, Go to Date and Print

    At home on any screen

    From a large monitor down to a phone-sized window, the layout adapts: the sidebar slides away, the views move to the bottom, and everything stays easy to use by touch or with the keyboard. Light or dark, it follows your system’s style and accent color.

    And much more

    The details that make a calendar pleasant to live in.

    • Repeating events

      Every weekday, the second Tuesday, the last Friday. Change one, this and the following, or all.

    • Reminders

      Several per event, with a snooze button. Missed ones are waiting when you come back.

    • Tasks

      Your to-do lists next to your events, from the same lists your other apps use.

    • Travel time

      Blocks out the trip before an event and tells you when it’s time to leave.

    • Weather

      The forecast under each day, with the high and the low, in °C or °F.

    • World clocks

      The time in other cities, and a second time zone next to the hours.

    • Time zones

      Give an event its own time zone and see it in yours, daylight saving included.

    • Search

      Start typing anywhere to find events by title, place or notes.

    • Activities search

      Find your events from the GNOME overview, without opening the app.

    • Copy and paste

      Copy a few events, click another day and paste: they keep their spacing.

    • Keyboard and screen readers

      Everything works from the keyboard, and the calendar describes itself to screen readers.

    • Runs in the background

      Close the window and reminders still arrive. Start at login if you like.

    Your calendar stays yours

    Your events live in GNOME’s calendar service on your computer, or with the accounts you chose — not on anyone else’s servers. There are no accounts to create, no ads and no tracking, and Navcal is free software, so anyone can check.

    Address suggestions and opening hours come from OpenStreetMap and forecasts from Open-Meteo. One switch in Preferences turns those lookups off.

    Questions

    Which calendars does it work with?

    Any account with calendars in GNOME’s Settings → Online Accounts: Google, Microsoft 365, Exchange, Nextcloud, and iCloud or any other CalDAV server. It also has calendars that stay on your computer, and can subscribe to calendar links (webcal or .ics).

    Can I use it alongside GNOME Calendar?

    Yes. Both use GNOME’s calendar service, so they show the same events, and a change in one shows up in the other — and in the clock menu in the top bar.

    Does it work outside GNOME?

    It works best on GNOME. On other desktops it needs GNOME’s calendar service (evolution-data-server) installed, and online accounts are added with GNOME Settings.

    Is it on Flathub?

    Not yet. For now, download the Flatpak file from the Download page — the GNOME runtime it needs still comes from Flathub, by itself.

    Does it need the internet?

    Only to sync your online calendars, and for the weather, address suggestions and opening hours. Turn off Look up places and weather online in Preferences and nothing else leaves your computer.

    Why don’t I see opening hours for a place?

    They come from OpenStreetMap, where not every place lists them yet. Pick the place from the suggestions while typing, so Navcal looks up the right one. You can add missing hours on openstreetmap.org, and everyone who uses the map gets them too.

    Will reminders come when the window is closed?

    Yes: closing the window keeps Navcal running in the background, and Start at login in Preferences starts it hidden when you log in. Reminders that came due while it wasn’t running are waiting next time, marked as missed.

    How do I back up my events?

    In Preferences → Calendars, choose Export Events… and “All calendars”. That makes one standard .ics file with everything, which Import Events… brings back — and which other calendar apps can read too.

    Which languages does it speak?

    English and French so far; quick add understands English phrasing. Every text in the app can be translated, and new languages are welcome.

    How can I help?

    Tell others about it, report problems and ideas, translate it into your language, or improve the code. The source comes with a guide for contributors.
