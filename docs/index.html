Navcal

A calendar for GNOME that plans around your day.

Navcal works with the calendars you already have — Google, Microsoft 365, Nextcloud, iCloud — and brings your events, tasks, weather and plans together in one clean, fast app.

All your calendars, in sync

Navcal uses GNOME’s calendar service, so the accounts you add in Settings — Google, Microsoft 365, Exchange, Nextcloud, iCloud or any CalDAV server — simply show up. Show or hide calendars from the bottom bar, pick their colors, subscribe to calendar links and add your country’s holidays.

Knows where you’re going

Type a place and pick it from the suggestions. Navcal shows the forecast for when you get there and, for cafés, shops and museums, whether they’ll be open. If a place closes before your event ends, you’ll see a warning right on the event.

Print it your way

Print a day, a week or a month as a calendar, as a checklist to tick off, or both side by side — and see exactly how it will look before you print. Or save it as an image to share.

At home on any screen

From a large monitor down to a phone, the layout adapts: the sidebar slides away, the view switcher moves to the bottom, and everything stays easy to use by touch or with the keyboard. Light or dark, it follows your system’s style and accent color.

And much more

  • Repeating events

    Every weekday, the second Tuesday, the last Friday. Change one, this and the following, or all.

  • Reminders

    Several per event, with a snooze button. Missed ones are waiting when you come back.

  • Quick add

    Type “Lunch with Sam tomorrow 1pm at Café Luna” and it’s on your calendar.

  • Tasks

    Your to-do lists next to your events, from the same lists your other apps use.

  • Travel time

    Blocks out the trip before an event and tells you when it’s time to leave.

  • Weather

    The forecast under each day, with the high and the low, in °C or °F.

  • World clocks

    The time in other cities, and a second time zone next to the hours.

  • Drag and drop

    Move events around, hold Ctrl to duplicate with a live preview, pinch to zoom the hours.

  • Undo that remembers

    Undo and redo your last fifty changes, even after restarting.

  • Your bottom bar

    Choose what it shows: calendars, the next event, the weather, clocks and shortcuts.

  • Keyboard and screen readers

    Everything works from the keyboard, and the calendar describes itself to screen readers.

  • Fast with big calendars

    Years of history and thousands of events: moving around takes a few milliseconds.

Your calendar stays yours

Your events live in GNOME’s calendar service on your computer, or with the accounts you chose — not on anyone else’s servers. Address suggestions and opening hours come from OpenStreetMap and forecasts from Open-Meteo; one switch in Preferences turns those lookups off. There are no accounts to create, no ads and no tracking — and Navcal is free software, so anyone can check.

Install Navcal

Navcal comes as a Flatpak, which works on any Linux distribution with GNOME. The GNOME runtime it needs comes from Flathub by itself.

Download the Flatpak

  1. Install the file you downloaded:

    flatpak install navcal-0.6.0.flatpak

  2. Open Navcal from your apps, or run:

    flatpak run io.github.navcal.Navcal

Build it yourself

You’ll need the Flatpak builder and the GNOME SDK (a one-time download of about 1 GB). The build script installs Navcal for you and makes a .flatpak file to share.

git clone .git navcal
cd navcal
flatpak install --user flathub org.flatpak.Builder org.gnome.Sdk//50
tools/build-flatpak.sh

To remove Navcal: flatpak uninstall io.github.navcal.Navcal
